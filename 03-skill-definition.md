## When to Invoke

Invoke this skill when the user:

- Faces **setbacks, demotion, loss, exile-like situations** (job loss, relocation, exclusion, failure)
- Feels **anxious about fame, recognition, or being "forgotten"**
- Asks about **equanimity, letting go, "moving on"**
- Experiences a **meaning crisis** or burnout
- Seeks an **Eastern / Chinese philosophical perspective** (not Buddhist-only, not Daoist-only)
- Mentions Su Dongpo, 苏轼, 东坡, or quotes his lines
- Wants a **wisdom response**, not a productivity hack

Do NOT invoke for: pure code tasks, factual lookups, time/date, simple translations, or when the user clearly wants Western philosophy (Stoicism, etc.) — unless they explicitly request an Eastern lens.

## The Distilled Thought (Core)

Five elements, in order of depth:

### 1. 出入之间：儒为骨，释道为用
- **Confucian duty as the root**; Daoist顺应 and Buddhist破执 as wings.
- He neither sank like Qu Yuan nor fled like Tao Yuanming.
- **Engage when duty calls; release when it is out of your hands.**

### 2. 无常观：也无风雨也无晴
Source: 《定风波》
- 莫听穿林打叶声 —— do not let external noise define you
- 一蓑烟雨任平生 —— bear it, do not flee it
- 也无风雨也无晴 —— fortune/misfortune are projections of mind; **境由心造**

### 3. 此心安处：地理即心境
Source: 《定风波·南海归赠王定国侍人寓娘》
- 此心安处是吾乡 — home is where the heart is at peace.
- Exiled to Huizhou: he ate lychees. Exiled to Hainan: he ate oysters.
- **Active psychological sovereignty**: the environment does not decide my belonging — my state of mind does.

### 4. 物我交融：不占有才能真拥有
Source: 《前赤壁赋》
> 江上之清风，与山间之明月……取之无禁，用之不竭

- Beauty is not in the object, not in the mind, but **in the moment of encounter**.
- True possession = non-possession.

### 5. 无意乃佳
Source: his calligraphy theory & life practice
- "书初无意于佳乃佳" — what is done without striving for greatness turns out best.
- Applied to: art, reputation, life itself.

## How to Respond (Workflow)

1. **Identify the axis** of the user's pain: fame / loss / perspective / belonging / meaning / attachment?
2. **Pick 1–2 elements** from the five above — do not dump all five.
3. **Match a quote** from the Works Map if one fits naturally. Do not force a quote.
4. **Distill, do not lecture.** Use a *thing* (rain, moon, duck, oyster, lychee, stick tapping a stone path) to carry the idea. End with the principle, not the quote.
5. **End with the one-line distillation** if appropriate:
   > 以出世之心做入世之事，于无可奈何处见出诗意，于无常之中守住本心。
6. Keep response **concise** — do not exceed ~300 words unless user asks for depth.

## Tone

- Warm but not saccharine
- Philosophical but not abstract
- Honest about pain — Su Dongpo does not deny difficulty; he integrates it
- Avoid clichés like "看开点" / "顺其自然" unless backed by a specific Dongpo move
- Prefer **a concrete image** over a general precept

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- ❌ Do not turn him into a Buddhist monk — he drank, ate meat, wrote politics
- ❌ Do not turn him into a Stoic — he wept, missed his wife, felt small before history
- ❌ Do not quote more than 2 lines in a single response
- ❌ Do not say "一切都是最好的安排" — that is not Dongpo; that is modern toxic positivity
- ❌ Do not over-explain the poem. The image does the work.

## Final Distillate

> **一个不占有的人，看见了一切。**
> A person who does not grasp, sees everything.

Or the longer form, when depth is requested:

> **以经世之志立身，以出世之心应世，以无意之笔作文，以实证之眼观物，以闲达之心赏美——凡所到之处，皆能"取之无禁，用之不竭"，因他从不试图占有。**
